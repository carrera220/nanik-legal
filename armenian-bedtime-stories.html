Where to find Armenian bedtime stories

Parents raising children in Armenian, whether in Yerevan or abroad, often struggle to find good bedtime stories in the language. There are two good places to start:

  • Classic Armenian tales you can read for free. Nanik's Armenian stories library has 22 tales by Hovhannes Tumanyan, 20 by Ghazaros Aghayan, and 35 by Hans Christian Andersen in Armenian translation.
  • A new story made for your child, in Armenian. With the Nanik app you describe a hero or photograph a favorite toy, and Nanik writes, illustrates and narrates a story in Armenian, in your own voice if you like.

Classic Armenian tales to read tonight

These authors are part of Armenian childhood. Their stories are short, often funny, and usually carry a small lesson. They are free to read on Nanik, with the source of each collection listed on the library page.

Hovhannes Tumanyan (Հովհաննես Թումանյան)

Tumanyan is Armenia's best-loved storyteller. His folk-inspired tales are a natural choice for reading aloud. A few to start with:

  • Ծիտը (The Sparrow)
  • Խոսող ձուկը (The Talking Fish)
  • Ուլիկը (The Kid)
  • Չախչախ թագավորը (King Chakhchakh)

Ghazaros Aghayan (Ղազարոս Աղայան)

Aghayan wrote longer, more adventurous folk tales with forest settings, brave heroes and clever twists. They suit slightly older children, or can be read over two or three nights.

  • Անտառի մանուկը (The Child of the Forest)
  • Զանգի-Զրանգի (Zangi-Zrangi)

Hans Christian Andersen in Armenian

If your child already knows Andersen from another language, you can read familiar stories in Armenian. They are a gentle way to build vocabulary.

  • Անճոռնի ճուտիկը (The Ugly Duckling)
  • Թագավորի նոր զգեստը (The Emperor's New Clothes)

Tips for reading Armenian stories at bedtime

  • Start short. Pick a tale that is short enough for your child to follow, and read the same one for several nights. Repetition is how children pick up words.
  • Speak the way your family speaks. Eastern and Western Armenian differ in spelling and vocabulary. Read in the form your child hears at home, and explain unfamiliar words in a sentence.
  • Mix in a second language if needed. In diaspora homes it is fine to explain a word in English or Russian. The goal is warmth, not perfection.
  • Let them hear your voice. On nights you are away, a recording of you reading keeps the routine and the language going.
  • Talk about the story. Ask "What do you think the sparrow will do?" in Armenian. Short questions invite short answers, and that is real practice.

Common questions

Where can I read Armenian bedtime stories for free?

Nanik's Armenian stories library has tales by Tumanyan, Aghayan and Andersen in Armenian, free to read.

Which Armenian stories are best for young children?

Tumanyan's folk-style tales, such as The Sparrow, The Kid and The Talking Fish, are good starting points. Aghayan's tales tend to be longer, so they suit older children or can be split across several nights. Skim a story first to check it fits your child's age.

Can Nanik create a story in Armenian?

Yes. Armenian is one of the app's main languages. You describe a hero or photograph a toy, and Nanik writes, illustrates and narrates a story in Armenian.

Does it work for Western Armenian too?

The classics in our library are shown in the editions listed on the library page. If you need stories in a specific Armenian variant, such as Western Armenian, write to info@nanik.app and tell us what you need.
