Bedtime stories when a parent is away

How to keep bedtime stories going when you travel, work nights or live apart, and how your child can still hear your voice.

Last updated October 24, 2026.

The short answer

You can keep bedtime stories going when you are not home. Each approach has a different feel:

  • A live video call is the closest to being there, but it depends on time zones and signal.
  • A recordable storybook lets you record your voice on each page of a physical book. It is a lovely keepsake, but it is one book, and you have to record every page.
  • Recordings you make in advance (voice memos, video) work for a favorite book, but you must record each story.
  • An app that tells new stories in your cloned voice, such as Nanik, lets you record a short sample once and your child hears a new story in your voice each night, even if you cannot be on the phone.

Many families combine them: a video call when possible, and a story in your voice on the nights it is not.

Why your voice matters

For a young child, a parent's voice is a sign of safety. Hearing it at bedtime helps them relax, even when you are a long way away. That is true for parents who travel for work, work night shifts, serve away from home, live apart after a separation, or are in a different country from the family.

Tips for bedtime when you are away

  • Keep the routine the same. Same order every night, even if someone else runs it. A familiar sequence is calming.
  • Tell your child when you will be back. Use something concrete, such as "after five sleeps". Children understand days better than dates.
  • Make a short goodnight message. Record a few sentences ("I love you, I am thinking of you, see you soon") that the caregiver can play at the end of the story.
  • Create a shared ritual. A phrase, a song or a gesture that you do at the same time from far away.
  • Read the same story. If you cannot read to them, you can both read the same book and talk about it on the next call.
  • Include the caregiver. Make it easy for whoever is at home: a short list of the routine and the stories to use.
  • Be honest about missing each other. It is fine to say "I miss you too". A story about a character who misses someone can help.

A story about missing someone

Sample story: Ruby and the Moonbeam Message

About a 3-minute read. About missing a parent who is away. Adapt the name and details for your child.

Ruby was a little otter who lived in a cozy burrow by the river. Her father was a boatman, and sometimes his work took him far away, up the river and over the hills, for many nights.

This week, Ruby's father was away. Ruby loved her mother, and she loved her blanket, and she loved her bedtime story. But tonight the burrow felt very quiet, and her tummy felt a little empty.

"Mama," she whispered, "I miss Papa."

Her mother sat beside her. "I miss him too," she said. "Missing someone is a sign of how much we love them. Come with me. I want to show you something."

They went to the window. The moon was full, and a silver beam of light stretched across the river and up into the hills.

"That moonbeam reaches all the way to where Papa is," said her mother. "The same moon shines on him tonight. When you look at it, he can look at it too."

Ruby looked. The moonbeam glowed softly, like a path. "Can I send him a message?" she asked.

"Of course. Whisper it to the moon."

Ruby took a breath and whispered, "Goodnight, Papa. I love you. I drew you a picture. Come home soon."

The moonbeam shimmered, as if it had heard. Ruby felt a warm glow in her tummy, where the empty feeling had been.

Her mother tucked her in and said, "Now, I will tell you a story, and while I do, think of Papa listening, too."

Ruby snuggled down. She could still see the moon through the window, and she knew that far away, her father was looking at it too.

Five sleeps later, there was a knock at the door. It was Papa, with muddy boots, a big smile, and a hug that lifted Ruby right off the floor.

"Did you get my message?" Ruby asked.

Her father laughed. "I did. The moon told me. It was the best message I have ever had."

That night, Ruby slept soundly, with her father in the next room, and the moon smiling through the window.

The end.

Common questions

How can I read bedtime stories to my child when I travel?

Use a mix of live video calls, recordings you make in advance, recordable books, or an app that tells new stories in your cloned voice, like Nanik. Keep the routine and the time the same, and tell your child when you will be back.

Can my child hear my voice when I am not there?

Yes. With a voice-cloning app you record a short sample once and the app can narrate new stories in your voice. With recordable books or voice memos you record each story yourself.

What if my child is upset when I am away?

Acknowledge the feeling, keep routines steady, and use a story about missing someone. If distress is intense or lasts for weeks, talk to your child's doctor.

Is it OK to use a cloned voice for my child?

Many parents find it comforting. Only clone your own voice or one you have permission to use, and read how the app handles recordings. See our page on voice cloning and privacy.
