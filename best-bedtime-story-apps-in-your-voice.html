Best bedtime story apps in your own voice

Several apps now let a parent or grandparent narrate bedtime stories without reading them aloud every night. Here is what each one does and who it suits.

Last updated October 24, 2026.

The short answer

If you want your child to hear a story in your own voice, there is no single best app. It depends on what you need:

  • A new personalized story every night, in many languages: Nanik.
  • Rhymes, songs and affirmations in your voice: Fabler.
  • Your own recordings of existing storybooks, plus AI stories: Snoozly.
  • A grandparent or relative reading from far away: Storibee or Gramms.
  • A very large library of ready-made voices: SleepyVoice.

Disclosure: we make Nanik. We have tried to describe the other apps fairly using what they state in their own App Store and Product Hunt listings. We have not tested them all in depth, and apps change often, so check each listing for current features, prices and privacy terms.

How to choose

Does the voice need to be yours?

Most parents want their own voice for the comfort of familiarity. Some want a grandparent or relative who lives elsewhere. Check that the app lets you save more than one named voice if that matters to you. Nanik, for example, supports several family voices.

Do you want new stories or recordings of existing ones?

Some apps focus on recording you reading favorite books. Others generate a new story each night. Apps that generate stories let you shape them around your child, their interests and what happened that day.

What language does your family speak?

Language support varies a lot. Some apps are English-only, while others cover dozens of languages. If you speak Armenian, Russian or another language at home, confirm it is supported for both the story and the narration. Nanik supports 100+ languages; see the full list.

What happens to the voice recording and photos?

Voice cloning and children's photos are sensitive. Read each app's privacy policy and ask whether the raw recording is kept, whether it is used to train models, and how to delete your data. Nanik's policy is on our privacy page.

Common questions

Can an app read bedtime stories in my own voice?

Yes. Several apps, including Nanik, let you record a short sample and then narrate stories in a voice created from it, so you do not have to record each story.

Can a grandparent record a voice for my child?

Yes, in apps that support it. Storibee and Gramms focus on this, and Nanik lets you save several named family voices.

Is it safe to give an app a recording of my voice?

It depends on the app. Look for clear statements on how long recordings are kept, whether they are used to train models, and how you can delete them. Read each privacy policy before recording.

Which apps support languages other than English?

It varies. Storibee currently lists English only, SleepyVoice lists 40 languages, and Nanik supports more than 100. Confirm your language on the app's own listing.
