Where to find Russian bedtime stories

Russian-speaking parents, whether in Russia, Armenia, Ukraine, Kazakhstan or abroad, have two good places to start:

  • Classic Russian tales. Folk tales and poems that generations of children grew up with, widely available in books and audio.
  • A new personalized story in Russian. With the Nanik app you describe a hero or photograph a favorite toy, and Nanik writes, illustrates and narrates a story in Russian, with your child's name in it. Russian is one of the app's main languages, alongside English and Armenian.

Classic Russian tales for bedtime

These are well-known Russian stories, easy to find in print or audio. They are listed here as suggestions, not hosted on Nanik.

Russian folk tales (русские народные сказки)

  • «Колобок» (The Little Round Bun): a short tale about a bun that runs away, good for ages 2 to 5.
  • «Репка» (The Giant Turnip): a repeating, cumulative story that toddlers love to join in.
  • «Теремок» (The Little House): animals take turns moving in, with an easy rhythm.
  • «Курочка Ряба» (The Speckled Hen): very short, ideal for the youngest children.
  • «Маша и медведь» (Masha and the Bear): a longer adventure for ages 4 and up.

Poems and longer tales

  • Alexander Pushkin's verse tales, such as «Сказка о царе Салтане», suit older children or can be read over several nights.
  • Korney Chukovsky's rhyming stories, such as «Мойдодыр» and «Тараканище», are fun, but are better for daytime than bedtime because the rhythm is lively.
  • Agniya Barto's and Samuil Marshak's short poems make easy wind-down reading.

Check a book's edition and age range before you buy. Some modern editions simplify or change the original text.

Tips for keeping Russian alive at bedtime

  • Make it the bedtime language. Even if your child speaks another language during the day, a Russian-only bedtime gives steady exposure.
  • Read the same tale many nights. Cumulative tales like «Репка» teach vocabulary through repetition.
  • Let them finish the sentences. Pause and let your child say the next word.
  • Use your own voice. Hearing a parent's Russian is the strongest signal that the language matters in your family. On nights you are away, a recording keeps it going.
  • Mix languages if you need to. In multilingual homes it is fine to explain a word in another language.
