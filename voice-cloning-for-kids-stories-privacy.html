Is voice cloning safe for kids' bedtime stories?

What happens to your voice recording, what to ask any app before you record, and what Nanik says about its own practices.

Last updated October 24, 2026.

The short answer

Voice cloning for children's stories can be done responsibly, but it involves sensitive data: your voice, and often information about your child. Whether an app is safe depends on what it collects, who processes it, how long it keeps it, and whether you can delete it. This page explains how voice cloning works, what to ask any app, and exactly what Nanik says about its own practices.

Note: this is a plain-language summary of Nanik's Privacy Policy (effective July 16, 2026) and Support page. The policy is the authoritative document. If anything here differs from it, the policy applies. We are not lawyers, and this is not legal advice.

How voice cloning for stories works

You record a short voice sample, in Nanik's case about 9 to 10 seconds. A voice-cloning model turns that sample into a synthetic voice that sounds like you. When you create a story, a text-to-speech system reads the story aloud using that voice. The result is a story narrated in your voice, without you recording each one.

This needs your recording to be processed by AI services, because the cloning and narration models are not run on your phone. That is the reason the questions in this page matter.

What Nanik says it collects and does

From Nanik's Privacy Policy and Support page:

  • Voice recordings. Voice recordings you submit are used to create a cloned storytelling voice. When you save a voice, you give it a name and tick a consent checkbox. Nanik's Support page says to only record a voice you have the right to use.
  • Child profile data. Information you choose to enter, such as name, age, interests and story preferences.
  • Photos. Photos you choose to submit as a story hero, for example a photo of a toy, are used to generate cover art.
  • Who processes the data. Voice samples, photos and story prompts are sent to the relevant AI or media providers only to fulfill the feature you request. Nanik also uses Supabase for authentication and backend services.
  • No sale of personal information. The policy states that Nanik does not sell personal information.
  • No cross-app advertising tracking. Nanik says it does not use the App Tracking Transparency advertising identifier for cross-app tracking. It does use analytics and crash diagnostics (for example Firebase) to improve the app.
  • Narration audio. The policy says library stories synced to your account include text and cover URLs, and that narration audio files are typically kept on your device.
  • Who the app is for. Nanik is designed for parents and guardians to operate. It is not a Kids Category app that children use alone.

Your controls

  • Permissions. Microphone, camera and photo access are optional and can be denied or changed any time in your phone's Settings. Without the microphone you can still choose a storyteller style instead of your own voice.
  • Deleting your account. If you are signed in with Apple, you can delete your account and associated server data from the Account screen in the app. Guest users can clear local data on the device.
  • Subscriptions are separate. Deleting your account does not cancel an App Store subscription. Cancel that in your Apple account settings.
  • Requests. For access, correction, deletion or other rights, email info@nanik.app.

What the policy does not specify

The policy says retention periods vary by data type and legal requirements. It does not publish a fixed number of days for how long voice samples are stored, and it does not say in a single sentence whether samples are used to train third-party models. If these details matter to you, ask us at info@nanik.app before you record, and ask any other app the same questions.

Questions to ask any voice-cloning app

  1. Is my raw recording kept, and for how long? Look for a clear retention period or a statement that it is deleted once the voice is created.
  2. Is my voice or my child's data used to train AI models? You want a clear no, or a way to opt out.
  3. Which companies process the recording? A good policy names categories of providers, and says what they may do.
  4. Can I delete my voice and my data? Check that deletion exists inside the app, not only through email.
  5. Does the app check that I have the right to use the voice? A consent step reduces the risk of someone cloning another person's voice.
  6. Is it built for parents or for children? Apps meant for adults to operate generally carry fewer child-data obligations than apps children use alone.
  7. Is my child's photo needed? Prefer apps that let you use a toy or a drawing instead of a face.

Practical tips for parents

  • Only clone your own voice, or a voice you have clear permission to use. That includes grandparents and other relatives: ask first.
  • Use a first name or nickname for your child. A story works just as well without a full name.
  • Use a toy photo, not a face photo. Nanik lets you use a favorite toy as the story hero.
  • Try the free plan first. You can create a voice and a few stories before deciding. Nanik's free plan includes 1 voice clone and 3 personalized stories.
  • Talk to older children about it. Explain that the story voice is a recording of you, so that they understand what a cloned voice is.

Common questions

Is it safe to record my voice for a bedtime story app?

It depends on the app. Check how long recordings are kept, whether they train AI models, who processes them, and whether you can delete them. Nanik's policy covers several of these points, and the sections above summarize them.

Can someone clone my voice without my permission?

A consent step helps. In Nanik, saving a voice involves naming it and ticking a consent checkbox, and the Support page says to only record a voice you have the right to use. Never share recordings of your voice publicly if you do not want them copied.

Does Nanik sell my data?

Nanik's Privacy Policy states that it does not sell personal information.

Can I delete my voice and account?

Yes. If you are signed in with Apple, you can delete your account and associated server data from the Account screen in the app. You can also email info@nanik.app.

Do I have to use my own voice?

No. You can choose a storyteller style inspired by classic children's authors instead. These are storytelling styles, not celebrity or actor voices.
